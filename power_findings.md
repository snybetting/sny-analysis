Question: how many bets do I need before an ROI figure is precise enough to make decisions on?

Method: Monte Carlo simulations
This method drew synthetic bets from my real odds distribution, it set a known true edge of 10% then randomly determined winners and computed ROI. 2,000 simulations at six different sample sizes.

Results: 
Bets      95% range        Width
1,000     2.75 – 18.25     15.5
2,600     4.99 – 15.37     10.4
5,000     6.49 – 14.07      7.6
10,000    7.51 – 12.64      5.1
20,000    8.08 – 11.93      3.9
50,000    8.87 – 11.14      2.3

What does this mean? At 2,600 bets, a genuine +10% ROI bettor lands anywhere between 5% and 15.4%. At 50,000 bets - around 38 years at my current rate - it's still 8.9% to 11.1%. There is no sample size I will reach where ROI becomes precise enough to detect a moderate change in edge.

Why does this matter? This is why CLV is the working metric and ROI can't be the main benchmark. The CLV difference test in Q3 gave a 1.5-point interval on 1,326 bets; ROI needed 50,000 bets to get to 2.3. This isn't my preference, it's a measured difference.

Caveat: The simulation assumes bets are independent. Mine aren't, I sometimes take multiple lines on one match - so the real intervals are somewhat wider than shown here.
